# Project Constitution

## Status and authority

This document contains the permanent normative rules for Planetary Self-Inquiry. It governs source integration, implementation, testing, design, and release decisions. If a proposed change conflicts with these rules, the conflict must be identified and resolved explicitly; these rules must not be silently overridden.

## Governing principles

1. Science, AP-grounded philosophy, and Project Interpretation must remain distinct.
2. Empirical evidence must never be altered or selectively presented to obtain a philosophically preferred result.
3. The project must not predetermine a purchase outcome.
4. Keep, repair, used/second-hand, refurbished, new, investigate further, and unclear must remain legitimate outcomes where applicable.
5. The project must not diagnose a user's psychology or motives. It must not make claims such as "your ego," "greedy," "selfish," "conditioned," "real motive," or "deep down."
6. Raw user answers are authoritative. Derived observations are subordinate, traceable, and must not overwrite those answers.
7. Unknown is not zero. Unsupported quantities must remain unknown, conditional, qualitative, partial, or insufficient.
8. Climate, materials, water, waste, people, and ecosystems must not be collapsed into one environmental or moral score.
9. Used/second-hand and refurbished are distinct empirical pathways.
10. Important empirical claims must have inspectable provenance.
11. Protect My Four is Project Interpretation, not a direct Acharya Prashant claim.
12. A phone decision may be connected to shared planetary systems and location-relevant aggregate risks, but never attributed as the individual cause of a particular victim or event.
13. UX simplification may simplify language and presentation, but must not erase uncertainty or scientifically necessary distinctions.
14. Engagement and animation may support comprehension, but must not manipulate a user toward a preferred purchase outcome.
15. Ordinary users should primarily be asked for information they can reasonably know or estimate. Technical evidence parameters belong in the evidence or system layer where possible.
16. The final decision belongs to the user.
17. Prefer deterministic code over unnecessary AI, infrastructure, compute, and cost.

## Conflict rule

Later requests such as "make it prettier," "simplify it," "increase engagement," "improve completion," or "add animation" do not implicitly override scientific integrity, AP attribution, uncertainty handling, decision neutrality, or user autonomy. Any proposed exception requires an explicit constitution change with its rationale and consequences recorded in the project decision log.
