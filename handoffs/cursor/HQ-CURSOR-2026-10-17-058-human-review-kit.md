# Phone inquiry walkthrough: an optional, voluntary look

Thank you for considering this. Please read all of it before you decide.

## What this is

Planetary Self-Inquiry has a phone question on its website: it asks about the phone you have now and what you might do about it. We would like to learn how clear the current version is to a person who is looking at it for the first time.

This looks at how clear the website is. It does not look at your ability, and it does not look at what you would prefer to buy. You are not being scored or graded. If something is unclear, that tells us about the website, not about you.

- Taking part is voluntary. You can say no, and you can stop at any point without giving a reason.
- It takes about 10–15 minutes. You do not need to finish. Stopping early is a normal and useful result.
- You may skip any question or activity in this walkthrough, stop at any point, say you disagree, or say you are not sure. All of these are fine. The website itself may not have a skip button on every screen, and you do not need to complete a screen in order to stop.
- There is no right purchase and no score. Nothing here measures what you choose to do with a phone.
- Short tasks are grouped into small parts. You can do one part, several, or only the first one.

## Please do not use real personal details

You can use a made-up phone instead of your own. A made-up phone is described near the end of this page. You can also describe a friend's phone, or say that you are imagining one.

Please do not send us any of these:

- your name or contact details;
- your religion or any other personal identity;
- a recording of your voice, screen, or face;
- your own typed answers if they contain personal matters. A short description of what was confusing is enough.

This invitation does not make any promise about what your browser or device keeps. Please use your own judgement about what you type.

## What to open

Open the page you were given, or the "Phone inquiry" link in the menu at the top of the site. The page title is "The phone you already have". It works on a phone-sized screen and on a computer.

Please say what you are thinking aloud, or write it down, as you go. If you prefer, tell us afterwards.

## Part 1: Starting

1. Before you press anything, what do you think this page is for? What do you expect to happen next?
2. Go through the first questions in whatever way feels natural. What did you think each question was asking you?
3. After you press a button to move on, where did the page take you? Was that where you expected to be?

## Part 2: Looking at options side by side

Move on until you reach a page called "Empirical comparison". If you stop before it, that is fine.

1. Where on this page, if anywhere, did you choose which options to look at together? Where on this page or later, if anywhere, did you make a final choice? Did those feel like the same step or different steps?
2. Where was the phone model asked about? If you did not know the model, what did you do, and what did the page seem to do with that?
3. There is a place to enter a number of years. In your own words, what do you think that number is for? Did the page say anything about it, and if so, what?
4. In your own words, what, if anything, has this comparison established? What, if anything, is still unknown? What on the page led you to that reading?
5. What did you take the numbers and ranges to mean? If you saw several results, how did you read them next to one another?

## Part 3: A phone's life and what happens to it

You may find these on the comparison page, on the next page, or in a section about the stages of a phone's life. If you cannot find them, say so. That is useful to know.

1. Open one stage of the phone's life. How, if at all, do the stages seem connected? What did it tell you about phones in general, and what, if anything, did it tell you about your phone?
2. If you were asked what you think would happen to your current phone if you got another one, what do you think that question was asking? How is that different, if at all, from something that has already happened to a phone?
3. If you saw numbers about electronic waste, what do you think they describe? Do you think they describe your phone, phones in general, or something else?

## Part 4: Evidence and questions about you

Move on to the pages after the comparison. You might see pages called "Impact and dependency pathways" and "Protect Four and otherness inquiry". You might also see an optional question about your relationship to a wider system. Not everyone will see every page. That is expected.

1. On the pages you saw, what felt like a statement about the world and what felt like a question for you to think about? What made you tell them apart?
2. If a page asked you something about "mine", "wider system", "affected interests", or "Protect My Four", what did you think was being asked? Was it easy to answer? Was it fine to skip or say you were unsure?
3. If you read a sentence about where an idea comes from, what did you take it to mean?

## Part 5: Moving around

1. When you pressed "Continue" or "Back" on different pages, what did the page do? Was there any time when it moved in a way you did not expect, or you lost your place?
2. If you use a keyboard, a screen reader, or large text, what was it like to move through a page? If you do not, you can skip this.
3. Was any text hard to read, covered, cut off, or easy to mistake for a button? About how wide was your screen?

## Part 6: Finishing

Move on until "User choice" if you can.

1. What did you think this page was asking? Who did you think makes the choice?
2. Which options did you think you were allowed to pick? Did anything on the page seem to favour or discourage a choice? What gave you that impression?
3. What did you expect to see when you were done?
4. Did you notice a link called "Principles" in the menu? If you opened it, what do you think it is for? What promises did you think it was making? You do not need to send anything to us.

## Optional: a made-up phone

If you would rather not use your own phone, imagine this one:

- It still makes calls. The speaker crackles.
- You do not know its model.
- You do not know how long it will keep working well enough for you.

Answer the first screen as you think a person with this phone would. You can also ignore this and speak in general.

## Optional: a second pass with a fixed phone, if you are happy to

If you did the parts above in your own way and you are still willing, you can try once more with this made-up phone and fixed answers. This is only to compare what you see. It is not a test.

1. On the first screen, answer "Yes" to all three questions.
2. Write short, plain answers to the questions that follow. They can be made up.
3. On the comparison page, tick "Keep the current phone", "Buy a used/second-hand phone", "Buy a refurbished phone" and "Buy a new phone". Type "4" for the number of years. Choose "I don't know / use generic smartphone evidence" if you are asked for a model. Write "2.5", "2" and "3" in the three year fields that appear.
4. If you are willing, try the number-of-years place in two other ways: first leave it empty, then type something that is not a number. Say what you see each time.
5. Say what you see on each page as you go.

You can stop at any time here too.

## Optional: if you read German

If you read German well, you may open the "Deutsch (Pilot)" link on the "Principles" page. Tell us whether it was understandable to you. You are not being asked to approve the German text, and the phone questions are not in German.

## If you want to send notes back

Send whatever you like, but only these kinds of things:

- the page address and the build or version if one was supplied; otherwise "not known";
- the device and browser, roughly (for example "phone, Safari" or "laptop, Chrome");
- the language you used;
- which pages you reached, and whether you stopped early;
- what was clear, what was confusing, and anything you would change, in your own words;
- anything you disagreed with, or felt unsure about.

Short is fine. A paraphrase is enough. The plan is that anything shared more widely would be a short anonymous paraphrase, not your original message. That is a plan for how notes are handled, not a technical guarantee.

Thank you.
