## 3. A — CO₂ growth factor: trace, arithmetic and limits

### 3.1 Independent candidates actually found

**A1 — NOAA Climate.gov, “Climate change: atmospheric carbon dioxide”**, public CO₂ overview (as indexed at 2026-10-17; webpage direct open degraded).  
`https://www.climate.gov/news-features/understanding-climate/climate-change-atmospheric-carbon-dioxide`

Relevant indexed passage:
- 1960s: ~0.8 ppm/y average growth;
- 1980s: ~1.6 ppm/y;
- **2015–2024: ~2.6 ppm/year** average growth;
- modern last ~60-year increase **100–200× faster than increase at end of last ice age, 11–17 ka ago** (a *different* geological comparison, period and denominator).

This is a NOAA public data summary referencing atmospheric observations and ice-core reconstructions. **2.6 is a recent 10-year average atmospheric *concentration* increment**, not the annual mass of anthropogenic emissions.

**A2 — IPCC Third Assessment Report WGI Ch. 3 §3.3.3, “Variations in Atmospheric CO₂ during the Past 11,000 Years”** (2001 legacy IPCC archive; indexed official primary synthesis; full direct opening returned an error).  
`https://archive.ipcc.ch/ipccreports/tar/wg1/index.php?idp=108`

The passage describes relatively small **natural net** Holocene variations, of order **20 ppm**, and a value near **260 ppm around 8 ka BP** rising towards **280 ppm before the industrial period**. The endpoints are approximate and the wording attributes causes as uncertain. This is **not** a published Holocene annual growth-rate distribution; it is a net difference across thousands of years using ice-core proxies.

**A3 — IPCC AR6 WGI Ch. 2 §2.2.3.2.1 (2021)**  
`https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/chapter/chapter-2/`

Official indexed section reports multi-centennial past CO₂ variability, examples of changes of a few ppm per century, and states **~125 ppm rise since 1850 over ~170 years is far larger than any comparable 170-year period inferred from last-800-ka ice-core records**, while explicitly noting **firn/ice bubble close-off and gas diffusion low-pass filtering**. No universal 1,040 ratio is given.

**A4 — IPCC AR6 WGI Ch. 5 “The Human Perturbation of the Carbon and Biogeochemical Cycles”, §5 / Figure 5.3 (2021)**  
`https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/chapter/chapter-5/`  
`https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/figures/chapter-5/figure-5-3/`

Official chapter/indexed summary: the rate of CO₂ increase over 1900–2019 was **at least 10× faster than any other time during the last 800,000 years** (**high confidence**) with explicit resolution/long-period caveats; the figure compares derivatives with sampling-resolution inserts. The ratio here is a **different metric**, not a bound asserting 1,040 is false under every possible historical comparison.

### 3.2 Reproducible *candidate reconstruction* — NOT scientific acceptance

Using independent rounded inputs A1/A2:

```text
Recent atmospheric CO2 concentration increment:
  ~2.6 ppm per year (2015–2024 NOAA decade average)

Approximate late-Holocene net endpoint change:
  ~280 ppm minus ~260 ppm = ~20 ppm
  time between ~8 ka BP and preindustrial = ~8,000 years (rounded)
  net slope = 20 ppm / 8,000 years = 0.0025 ppm/year

Ratio of the two selected and differently averaged slopes:
  (2.6 ppm/year) / (0.0025 ppm/year) = 1,040
```

**This exactly reproduces the claimed integer only under these choices.** It is a **post hoc explanatory hypothesis**, not a traced author equation, original-video bibliographic reference or modern uncertainty-qualified climate indicator.

Why `1,040× average Holocene growth` cannot be promoted:
1. **Modern numerator:** 2015–2024 ~2.6 ppm/y versus a single year, last 60 years, 1850–2025, or a 100-year mean yield different magnitudes.
2. **Historical denominator:** net ~20 ppm endpoint gain from approximately 8 ka to preindustrial is not the mean *absolute* rate of year-to-year, century-scale, or millennial CO₂ changes within all of the Holocene. Positive and negative fluctuations can cancel to a tiny net slope; near-zero or negative baselines make ratios unstable or undefined.
3. **Time definitions:** “Holocene average” could mean 11.7 ka-present, 8 ka–1750, preindustrial-only, a chosen millennium, last deglaciation (which is a different interval), or a selected ice-core resolution.
4. **Measurement systems:** direct well-mixed atmospheric monitoring is compared with ice-core gas histories smoothed by firn mixing/closure. Short-term historical maxima cannot be recovered at modern-resolution equivalence from a net difference.
5. **Precision:** `2.6`, `20`, `8,000`, `260` and `280` are rounded context figures with no propagated covariance, proxy uncertainties or harmonized baseline; the four-digit `1,040` implies unwarranted precision.
6. **Physical unit:** both selected numerator and denominator are concentration slopes (ppm/year); neither is emissions mass (GtCO₂/year), warming rate (°C/decade), nor a natural-sink entitlement. Dividing atmospheric growth by global anthropogenic emissions is invalid without carbon-cycle accounting.
7. **Source attribution:** no primary source identified which specifically defines exactly this `1,040` comparison and its uncertainty. The numerical match is **not proof** which data the questioner used.

### 4.1 Source-accurate quantities

| Value | What the original paper actually estimates | Method, period and limit |
|---|---|---|
| **1.37°C** | **Assessed human-induced global mean surface warming in calendar 2025** relative to **1850–1900**. | Best estimate with **likely range 1.1–1.7°C** (paper §8.1). *Single-year attributed estimate*, not the 2016–2025 decade average and not the annual observed trend. |
| **0.27°C per decade** | **Assessed rate of human-induced warming over 2016–2025**, not °C/year. | **Likely 0.2–0.4°C per decade**, §8.2/Table 7. Assessed via three attribution methods (Global Warming Index, kriging for climate change, regularised optimal fingerprinting); a rolling 10-year trend in attributed anthropogenic warming, distinct from observed trend. |
| **0.30°C per decade** | **Observed** warming decadal trend (2016–2025), calculated using observed datasets, §8.2. | Internal climate variability affects observed rates; paper cautions current close agreement with attributed 0.27 may be partly coincidental. **Not** a substitute for 0.27 on the human-attributed banner. |
| **1.39 ± 0.13°C** | **Observed** GMST in 2025 relative to 1850–1900, §7. | Not identical to human-attributed 1.37; observational uncertainty convention as supplied, not same as the 1.1–1.7 attribution likely interval. |
| **1.24 [1.0–1.5]°C** | **2016–2025 decadal mean human-induced** warming, relative to preindustrial. | A decade-average level, not single-year 1.37 nor rate. |
| **1.26 [1.13–1.36]°C** | **2016–2025 decadal mean observed** warming, relative to preindustrial. | A decade-average level with different uncertainty/reporting basis. |

The paper follows IPCC AR6 attribution frameworks, updating global observations and forcing inputs. Its §8.1 combines three methods for attribution; §8.2 estimates a rolling ten-year linear trend and reports a multi-method likely range. Natural variability affects the measured/observed series. **The rate (0.27) is not the rate of atmospheric CO₂ concentration rise**, nor a projection that temperature rises by 0.27°C each future decade.
