## Three selected examples — source, evidence and limits

### A. Glacier storage → changing river conditions

**AP source/claim:** The 17 Oct official op-ed's Himalaya passage above points from retreating snow/ice and expanding glacial lakes to flood hazard and longer-term variation in river water for farming, drinking, hydropower and economies. The article also discusses dramatic recent events and a philosophy of consumption; neither is necessary to establish the water mechanism. Its cited assessment is ICIMOD's 2026 glacier outlook, with a 2023 assessment for the 65% comparison.

**Independent check:** [ICIMOD 2026 report record](https://lib.icimod.org/records/fk7f9-2ha88) and [institutional release](https://www.icimod.org/press-releases/hindu-kush-himalaya-glaciers-losing-ice-at-double-the-rate-since-2000-new-icimod-report-confirm/) report roughly doubled ice loss after 2000 based on **38 monitored glaciers, only seven** meeting global benchmark monitoring standards. The [2023 HI-WISE executive summary](https://hkh.icimod.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2023/07/Executive__Summary_HKH-WISE-Report.pdf) reports 2000–09 average –0.17 to 2010–19 –0.28 metres water equivalent/year, 65% faster; methods and intervals differ from the 2026 comparison. [HI-WISE water/ice assessment](https://hkh.icimod.org/hi-wise/) describes changing availability and hazards. This supports cryosphere change and basin-dependent risks, not uniform glacier mass loss at every site, a guaranteed flood, precise downstream water volume or a deterministic consequence of one decision. River discharge also depends on monsoon rain, seasonal snow, groundwater, dams and withdrawals. Treat “nearly two billion supported” as a broad region/population framing, not two billion people drinking glacier melt directly.

**Dependency chain:** mountain snow/ice stores water → seasonal melt/lakes alter runoff and hazard → connected river basins affect downstream water, crops, infrastructure and livelihoods. **Possible PSI question (Project Interpretation only):** “Which conditions outside your immediate community help keep a familiar source of water or food reliable, and what remains uncertain about that connection?” **Visual:** simple seasonal storage/river-basin diagram, with rain and other sources shown; no disaster countdown or exact household risk.

### B. Heat exposure → potential work capacity → household resources

**AP source/claim:** The same 17 Oct op-ed says India's outdoor workers and farming households are exposed to heat, citing the Lancet Countdown's 2024 estimated **247 billion potential labour hours** and about **US$194 billion potential income** reduction; it describes family earnings pressure. This is not a measured payroll loss for every person.

**Independent check:** [Lancet Countdown 2025 India data sheet](https://lancetcountdown.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/India_Lancet-Countdown_2025_Data-Sheet-2.pdf), indicator 1.1.3, reports the 247 billion potential hours in 2024, **66% agriculture** and 20% construction; indicator 4.1.3 estimates US$194 billion *associated potential income* loss. The estimates combine heat exposure, work-capacity assumptions and economic modelling; they are national annual indicators, not observed lost wages, a solely anthropogenic-climate attribution, individual diagnoses or a prediction for a specific family. The physical heat/work constraint and sector exposure are well grounded; translating to any person's earnings requires occupation, scheduling, acclimatization, cooling, protections and local conditions.

**Dependency chain:** climate and local temperature/humidity → safe capacity or schedule of outdoor work → possible income and food-production pressure → household well-being. **Possible PSI question:** “What conditions allow the people who grow food or work outdoors to do that work safely, and who can change those conditions?” **Visual:** short day/work-schedule narrative or exposure-to-capacity diagram; mark the national figure as modeled potential and omit a fake per-person amount.

### C. Rock processing → respirable silica → worker health

**AP source/claim:** The 28 Dec official Aravalli op-ed contrasts finished building stone with the occupational dust generated during quarrying/crushing. It additionally alleges particular site, class, legal and water outcomes that are **not** established by the generic mechanism.

**Independent check:** [NIOSH worker-health guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/silica/about/index.html) establishes that cutting, grinding, polishing and drilling silica-containing material can release respirable crystalline silica that reaches the lungs and causes silicosis and other diseases. [NIOSH dust-control report for metal/nonmetal mining](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/engcontrols/ecd/detail164.html) covers rock cutting, crushing and transport; [ILO report on India's natural-stone sector](https://researchrepository.ilo.org/esploro/fulltext/report/Challenges-and-opportunities-for-productive-employment/995219490602676?institution=41ILO_INST&mId=13100794560002676&repId=12100730230002676) documents occupational hazards and under-reporting. These do **not** measure airborne concentrations or prove silicosis in a named Aravalli quarry or village. Rock mineralogy, task, ventilation, wet cutting and protective controls materially affect exposure; not all “dust” is crystalline silica. Do not infer that a particular buyer sourced a particular quarry. Calling this “climate injustice” collapses occupational health, distribution and climate categories; it is not a scientific label established here.

**Dependency chain:** building material demand and extraction → potentially hazardous dusty tasks → worker exposure if controls fail → possible long-term health consequences. **Possible PSI question:** “What work occurs before a finished material reaches us, and what evidence would we need to understand the conditions of that work?” **Visual:** production-stage process map with a control point and unknown supplier link, not a named victim or personal guilt cue.

## Recommendation and learning

**Recommend B then A for later comprehension/design evaluation.** B is near to ordinary work and family income with a directly traceable primary indicator; A is a good cross-border water mechanism but needs a basin/season illustration to avoid exaggeration. C is a useful Research reserve, not yet a specific PSI example. Scientific layer establishes conditional physical/social dependencies; AP's philosophical framing of denial, desire or responsibility is a separate attribution/context question; PSI may invite inquiry without adopting AP wording or inferring motives. The accepted Protect My Four question and evidence gate are unchanged.

**Owner learning — “potential hours” is not a time clock.** Heat-index models estimate how many hours of work capacity *could* be lost when people face unsafe heat under specified assumptions. Aggregating over millions of workers gives a large national number. It does not mean a payroll system counted 247 billion missed shifts or that climate change alone explains every hour. First identify exposure, then the modelled capacity effect, then the uncertain income and family consequences.

**Smallest next action:** if HQ wishes to evaluate B and A for possible future PSI interpretation, pass those exact article passages and provenance grades to AP Framework & Corpus for a bounded context/attribution review; separately test whether a short non-leading diagram/question improves visitor comprehension. Neither step grants publication or scientific adoption. Existing AP freshness schedule suffices; no ongoing discovery process is created.
