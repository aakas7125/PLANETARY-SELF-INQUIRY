# Heat, work, and the people we care about

**Offline story prototype · 26 October 2026 · for review, not public PSI copy**

## Visitor card

Picture food being harvested on a very hot day. For some outdoor workers, heat can make a usual pace unsafe. Work may slow, move to cooler hours, or pause. What happens next depends on the job, protection, and whether the work can be made up. If it cannot, earnings or food-growing work may come under pressure; this story does not claim a loss for any particular household or harvest.

**Evidence — what we know.** Heat can limit the capacity for outdoor work. The [Lancet Countdown's 2025 India data sheet](https://lancetcountdown.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/India_Lancet-Countdown_2025_Data-Sheet-2.pdf) estimates **247 billion potential labour hours** lost to heat exposure in India in 2024; 66% of that modelled national estimate falls in agriculture (indicator 1.1.3). Its associated income figure is also *potential*, not wages counted from pay records (indicator 4.1.3). These estimates do not tell us what happened on a given farm or to one family. Local heat, work hours, adaptation and protections matter.

**AP discussion — a separate source.** An [official article published under Acharya Prashant's byline on 17 October 2026](https://acharyaprashant.org/en/articles/climate-denialism-excuses-change-dishonesty-remains-acharya-prashant-on-the-pioneer-1_b38c61860) discusses India's outdoor work and the possible pressure of heat on earnings and families (body passage beginning “India has particular reasons to worry”). This is a paraphrase of an edited publication, not a verified spoken quotation. Its numerical claim is checked against the Lancet source above; the article itself does not establish an individual loss.

**PSI inquiry — your interpretation.** What, if anything, does this connection between outdoor working conditions and daily life suggest about the well-being of people you care about?

## Reviewer note — small voluntary comprehension check

Show only the visitor card to a first-time participant. Without explaining it, ask them to describe (1) the chain from heat to work and a *possible* household or food effect, (2) one uncertainty or condition that changes the outcome, and (3) which part is the Lancet evidence, which is the AP article, and which is PSI's question. Invite “I do not know” and disagreement. Record their words and any mistaken inference, without correcting during the first response, scoring agreement, asking about guilt, or measuring a preferred decision. A suitable response identifies a conditional link and the modelled status of the national figure; confusion about observed family losses or treating the PSI question as a scientific finding means the card needs revision. This is a proposed evaluation, not a completed participant test.
